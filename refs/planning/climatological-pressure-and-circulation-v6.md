# Climatological pressure and circulation v6

## Intent

Replace the expensive local gyre-packing model with a fast, legible climatological circulation model. The target is broad physical plausibility and useful worldbuilding causality, not fluid simulation.

The model should answer four coarse questions:

1. Where are the durable climatological pressure highs and lows?
2. Where do prevailing winds broadly originate and travel?
3. Where are subsidence, convergence, and likely storm-track corridors?
4. What basin-scale ocean circulation follows from those atmospheric and geographic constraints?

## Pressure systems

Pressure is resolved on a fixed `128x64` equirectangular reference grid. Its cost therefore remains bounded when output resolution increases.

The reference model contains:

- an equatorial trough / ITCZ-like low regime;
- subtropical oceanic highs near approximately 30 degrees north and south;
- subpolar lows near approximately 55-60 degrees;
- polar highs;
- weak broad thermal modulation over land;
- explicit `subsidencePotential`, `convergencePotential`, and `stormTrackPotential` fields;
- a prevailing-wind field derived from latitude bands and coarse pressure gradients.

Elevation is not treated as an atmospheric high. Terrain only applies bounded steering to the projected prevailing-wind field.

## Precipitation coupling

The first increment applies a bounded final projected-surface correction:

- convergence increases precipitation and moisture;
- storm tracks increase precipitation;
- subsidence suppresses precipitation and moisture;
- wetness and projected biome classification are reconciled after the correction.

This is intentionally shallow. It does not run an iterative atmosphere model.

### Known boundary

The current deep-time ordering builds authoritative topology hydrology before final raster projection and circulation. Therefore this increment's pressure correction changes projected precipitation, wetness, and biome presentation but does not yet reroute authoritative topology rivers. Moving the fixed-resolution pressure model into the topology climate pass is a later architecture increment and should be explicit rather than silently implied.

## Ocean circulation

The retired v5.1 implementation repeatedly rescanned the output raster while packing as many as fourteen local ellipses. That model was both expensive and biased toward small local swirls.

Version 6 instead:

1. detects broad open-ocean longitude sectors at subtropical and subpolar reference latitudes;
2. creates at most one gyre descriptor per meaningful sector and regime;
3. limits the total descriptor count to ten;
4. evaluates all descriptors analytically in one output-raster pass;
5. adds westward equatorial currents and an eastward countercurrent;
6. adds a circumpolar current only where a coarse high-latitude path is genuinely open;
7. applies western-boundary intensification and limited coastal steering;
8. smooths once without iterative ownership packing.

Northern subtropical gyres rotate clockwise and southern subtropical gyres counterclockwise. Subpolar gyres use the opposite sign.

The legacy diagnostic field names `packedGyres` and `gyreOwner` remain temporarily for debug-view compatibility. They now describe broad deterministic circulation territories rather than results of a packing search.

## Determinism and compatibility

- Same seed, settings, algorithm version, and resolution must reproduce identical pressure centers and vector fields.
- Current and wind signatures intentionally change from v5.1 to v6.
- Terrain, coastlines, water masks, plates, and unrelated geography are not regenerated by this increment.
- The pressure reference resolution is part of the algorithm-version contract, not the output-resolution contract.

## Performance contract

The removed operation is:

```text
basin-circulation.pack-gyres
```

New attributable operations are:

```text
climatological-pressure.build-reference-model
climatological-pressure.apply-fields
basin-circulation.build-large-scale-gyres
basin-circulation.evaluate-large-scale-field
```

The focused production rerank remains the acceptance gate. The expected result is a large reduction in the history-stage contribution previously dominated by gyre packing.

## Visual acceptance

Visual QA should verify:

- two to six dominant subtropical gyres on ordinary Earthlike worlds, where basin geometry permits;
- no carpet of small local swirls;
- broad trade-wind, westerly, and polar-wind organization;
- stronger western-boundary currents;
- legible equatorial currents;
- subtropical dry-zone pressure influence and mid-latitude storm-track influence;
- sensible behavior for fragmented Archipelago basins rather than one gyre per small sea.

Exact visual equality with Earth is not required. The requirement is a recognizably plausible large-scale circulation grammar.
